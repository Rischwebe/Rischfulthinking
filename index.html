I’m Collette Sinclaire Risch.

I grew up in Wisconsin, was literally raised as a witch, married my soulmate, Camden, and somehow ended up with nine kids, seven with special needs.

I’m autistic, an international adoptive mom, a psychic medium, an entrepreneur, an obsessive reader, an advocate, and a lifelong Diet Pepsi addict.

I’ve also been called weird for most of my life. Fine. I’ve always thought weird was just another way to say awesome. Admittedly, that is very convenient coming from the weird one.

Rischful Thinking is where I talk about family, autism, disability, adoption, mediumship, books, history, advocacy, people, things I can’t explain, things people explain very badly, and whatever else I’m thinking about at the moment.

I make absolutely no promise that these subjects will coordinate. They rarely do.

The inheritance

MY GRANDMOTHER WAS
THE WITCH OF ANTIGO.

This is an actual sentence about my family.

My grandmother lived in Antigo, Wisconsin, where she was known as the Witch of Antigo. She saw spirits. My mother saw spirits. I see spirits.

I’m a third-generation psychic medium. And I was literally raised as a witch. Not witchy. Not “we owned a crystal.” Actually raised with spirits, psychic experiences, intuition and witchcraft.

Other families passed down recipes. Mine passed down dead people.

So when I read Shirley MacLaine’s Out on a Limb in third grade, it wasn’t exactly a spiritual awakening. Apparently I was doing supplemental reading.

I’ve experienced things I cannot explain. I believe in spirits. I also think psychics can be completely full of shit. These beliefs get along just fine.

I don’t think every flickering light is Grandma. Sometimes you need a new bulb. Adding the word quantum to something does not automatically make it science.

“A spirit told me” is not a research citation.

Mystery is allowed. Bullshit is optional.

Family

NINE KIDS.

There really isn’t a casual way to say it.

Camden and I have nine children. Seven have special needs. Our family grew through birth and international adoption and is multiracial, neurodivergent, complicated, funny, occasionally completely nuts and very loved.

I’ll talk about motherhood, adoption, autism, schools, advocacy, the things we got right, the things we got spectacularly wrong, and probably quite a bit of: How the hell did this become my Tuesday?

Motherhood changed almost everything about me. My Diet Pepsi addiction survived beautifully.

Family →

AND THEN THERE’S CAMDEN.

Camden Risch is my husband and my soulmate.

Yes, soulmate. I realize that word can sound like something written over a sunset with two dolphins jumping through it. Unfortunately, it’s accurate.

We’ve built a family and businesses together. Raised nine kids together. After all these years, he is still my person. He also knows exactly how weird I am. There was no bait and switch.

Neurodivergence

I’M AUTISTIC.

Finding that out explained a lot. Not everything. Some of my weirdness remains independently owned and operated.

I’m literal. I can get intensely interested in things. I like quiet and being home. I’m perfectly happy in my own company and could probably live quite successfully as a hermit. I just don’t confuse being alone with being lonely.

I need actual sleep. Not four hours and a motivational quote. I don’t even drink coffee, so that plan was doomed from the beginning.

Why are we asking people to become easier for the system instead of asking the system to become better for people?

I CARE ABOUT PEOPLE.

This occasionally confuses people because I’m not particularly social. Those are not opposites.

I can care enormously about somebody without needing to have brunch with them. I hate gossip. But tell me somebody is being treated unfairly? Now I’m listening.

BOOKS HAVE ALWAYS
BEEN A PROBLEM.

Mainly a shelving problem.

I read constantly. My favorite author is Alice Hoffman. I love biographies, Gilded Age history and historical fiction, psychology, spirituality, human behavior and books that force me to find an entirely different book.

I have read hundreds of New Age and self-help books. Some changed my life. Some helped me survive. Some were beautiful.

Some contained one really good idea that probably could have been handled in twelve pages.

Sadly, the self-help pamphlet industry never really took off.

History

NOT HISTORICAL GOSSIP.

What fascinates me is how people lived and how the world around them worked: culture, class, architecture, labor, women, immigration, industry, power, social rules.

I especially love the Gilded Age. I can spend an unreasonable amount of time looking at an old staircase. This is apparently what adulthood brought me. Not coffee. Staircases.

DIET PEPSI.

Now we are discussing an actual lifelong commitment.

I have been addicted to Diet Pepsi for basically forever. Coffee has always seemed far too grown-up. And everybody is strangely committed to pretending coffee breath isn’t happening. It is happening.

I’ll keep the Diet Pepsi.

THE PART THAT
ISN’T FUNNY.

My childhood involved poverty, instability and abuse. I am a childhood trafficking survivor. I have lived with a severe lifelong eating disorder and serious mental-health struggles. My father was deeply racist and involved with the Ku Klux Klan.

Those things shaped me. But I refuse to make the worst things that ever happened to me the most interesting thing about me.

Some stories I may never tell. Some I can finally look at and say: Well. That was completely fucked.

WHY RISCHFUL THINKING?

It started as a joke about my last name. Then it annoyingly fit.

Wishful thinking: I want this to be true.

Rischful thinking: I want this to be true. Okay. But is it?

Who said it? How do they know? Did anybody check? Am I wrong? Entirely possible. Did seventeen websites copy the eighteenth website and now everybody thinks it’s a fact?

Now I need to know. And there goes the afternoon.
