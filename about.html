About Collette Risch

SALLY JEAN WAS INHERITANCE.
COLLETTE WAS INTENTION.

I’m 52, Wisconsin raised, autistic, a third-generation psychic medium, married to Camden, and the mother of nine. That is the short version. It leaves quite a lot out.

The person I am now

I’m Collette Sinclaire Risch. I like quiet, books, old staircases, Stevie Nicks, Blink-182 and Diet Pepsi. I care about people, although I would usually rather care about them without attending a crowded event.

I grew up entirely in Wisconsin, in a family descended from cotton sharecroppers, mill workers and waitresses, with deep poverty and little formal education in its history.

A witch in Wisconsin

My grandmother saw spirits and was known in Antigo as the Witch of Antigo. My mother saw spirits. I see spirits. I was literally raised as a witch, with mediumship, intuition and psychic experience treated as parts of the world.

I later spent decades developing my mediumship, with influences and work or training that included Sylvia Browne, John Holland and Sonia Choquette. I believe what I have experienced. I am also skeptical of certainty, bad evidence and psychics who are completely full of shit.

Becoming Collette

I was born Sally Jean Flowers. Changing my name was about survival, identity, reinvention and choosing who I wanted to become. Sinclaire was chosen partly for Upton Sinclair and his work on labor, exploitation and inequality. The spelling also contains “sin.” Obviously I noticed.

Camden and the family we built

Camden Risch is my husband and soulmate. Yes, soulmate. The dolphins can keep jumping over the sunset; it is still accurate. Together we built businesses and a family of nine children through birth and international adoption. Seven have special needs. Our family is multiracial, neurodivergent, complicated, funny and deeply loved.

Autism, systems and advocacy

Knowing I am autistic explained my literalness, intense interests and comfort with solitude. Raising children with significant special needs taught me the other side of disability systems: the forms, schools, assumptions and low expectations.

I care about who gets underestimated, ignored or treated as expendable. That reaches disability, race, adoption, LGBTQ+ and trans rights, women, survivors, children and animals.

The history I carry

My childhood included poverty, instability, abuse and human trafficking. I have lived with a severe lifelong eating disorder and serious mental-health struggles. My father, born in Pelham, Georgia, came from extreme poverty, left school after eighth grade, worked in a mill and became a union electrician. He was intelligent. He was also abusive, deeply racist and involved with the Ku Klux Klan. He is dead.

Poverty is context. It is not an alibi. These facts matter, but the worst experiences of my life do not get to become the most interesting things about me.

What this is

Rischful Thinking is an editorial home for family, autism, advocacy, mediumship, skepticism, books, Gilded Age history, architecture, psychology, human behavior and questions that refuse to stay in their assigned department.

I have been vegetarian since 17. I ran varsity cross-country in high school until my knee eventually objected. My favorite author is Alice Hoffman. These details may or may not be related. That is rather the point.
